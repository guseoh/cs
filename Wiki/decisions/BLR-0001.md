# Study CS systematically by subject and chapter, then revisit in projects

## Context

Project-led study can leave gaps when important computer science concepts do not arise during current implementation. The learning objective is a coherent university-style foundation, not only a collection of techniques encountered while coding.

## Decision

Study CS as an independent, structured curriculum organized by subjects and chapters. Topics include data structures and algorithms, computer architecture, operating systems, networks, databases and relevant infrastructure/distributed-systems fundamentals. Learn underlying mechanisms and relationships first; when a concept is required or reappears in a project, revisit and deepen it using the concrete code or operational issue.

Keep theoretical study material distinct from project-specific implementations, experiments and troubleshooting. Connecting records is useful, but the project is not the only source of the CS syllabus.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Project needs only:** not the chosen primary CS curriculum because it can omit foundational topics.
- **Every CS chapter must become a feature:** not required. Projects provide later applications and review opportunities, not the sole curriculum.

## Consequences and constraints

- Progress is measured through understanding rather than merely applying a tool in a project.
- Subject-by-subject order, textbook selection, hourly allocation and a mandatory quiz routine were **not decided** in this conversation.
- This decision does not modify files under `Study/**` or impose quizzes on Large-scale Board.

## Implementation/verification

Learning policy confirmed in the conversation on 2026-11-04. No claim of completed chapters, verified review schedule or project implementation.

## Provenance

Direct user clarification: CS is intended to be studied like a university major, chapter by chapter, then consulted again when relevant to development.
